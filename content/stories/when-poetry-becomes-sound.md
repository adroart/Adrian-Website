I have always written. Poetry that lives in notebooks, in margins, in the quiet after ceremony. Words that were never meant to be seen by many, just felt by me as a way of moving something through.

Recently, I began exploring what happens when those words are given a body. Not performed, not read aloud, but translated. Given melody, breath, texture, and time.

> Poetry has always been music that forgot its notes. This is the remembering.

Using AI as a compositional collaborator, I feed the poem and something emerges. Not always what I expected. Sometimes more than I could have made alone. The AI does not understand the words the way I do. But it responds to their rhythm, their weight, their space.

What I find is that the music reveals something the poem was already holding. A tone beneath the tone. A feeling that language points toward but cannot fully carry.

These are not produced tracks. They are experiments. First attempts at a language that is still forming. I share them here as they are, because that is the only honest thing to do.

If you listen, let the words land first. Then let the music do what words cannot.
