# 04 — The I-Ching Translation Method

How the classic layer of each card (the Judgment and the Image — see
`03_ICHING_GUIDE.md`, Layer 2) is rendered.

Read `00_MASTER_WRITING_GUIDE.md` and `03_ICHING_GUIDE.md` first. This document
governs one specific, consequential act: producing a **fresh modern-English
rendering** of the I-Ching's Judgment and Image for all 64 hexagrams, using
multiple scholarly sources and AI as a research instrument, with Adrian as the
final translator.

This is a serious scholarly act. The method exists to make it *careful* — to
produce a rendering more accurate than relying on any single old translation,
without committing the errors that an AI-assisted translation is most prone to.

**A note on the two phases.** Master guide §1 frames most of the deck as a
Phase 1 AI scaffold that Adrian later rewrites in Phase 2. The classic layer
is different. A translation is not a draft to be replaced by personal voice —
its standard is *accuracy*, not authorship, and once a line is rendered
correctly from the evidence it does not get "made more Adrian's." So here, the
method itself *is* the finished process: AI triangulates and drafts, Adrian
verifies and renders the final line, and that line is done. Phase 2 for the
classic layer means revisiting flagged forks or adding an expert review (§7),
not rewriting for voice. The intro and interpretation layers around it (Layer
1 and Layer 3 in `03`) follow the normal two-phase pattern.

---

## 1. The three dangers this method is built against

### Danger 1 — False confidence over genuine ambiguity

The I-Ching's text is Old Chinese, roughly three thousand years old. It is
genuinely ambiguous even to expert sinologists: characters carried different
meanings then, the grammar is compressed almost to telegraphy, and many lines
have no settled scholarly reading. An AI will translate any line into a
confident, fluent English sentence. **That fluency is the trap.** The original
is not fluent or settled; a smooth modern sentence can hide a real
3,000-year-old ambiguity by silently picking one side.

*Safeguard:* the method surfaces ambiguity rather than resolving it invisibly.
Every contested line is flagged, with the fork shown.

### Danger 2 — Modernizing the meaning, not just the English

"Aligned with the times" means **the English, never the meaning.** The
I-Ching's value is that it speaks from *outside* our moment. Importing modern
assumptions — therapeutic language, individualism, contemporary psychology —
into a Bronze Age divinatory text quietly changes what it says. That is
mistranslation wearing a friendly mask.

*Safeguard:* modern, clear, living English is the goal. A meaning bent toward
modern sensibility is a defect. Faithfulness to the Bronze Age sense is the
standard. (Incidental archaic social framing — "the superior man" — may become
neutral — "the noble one" — but the *counsel itself* is never altered.)

### Danger 3 — Synthesis as false consensus

When scholars disagree on a line, the disagreement is *information*: it tells
you the line is genuinely open. Blending readings into one synthesis can
manufacture a consensus that does not exist, or average five readings into
something none of the five scholars would endorse.

*Safeguard:* synthesis is not averaging. Where sources genuinely diverge, the
method records the divergence and Adrian makes a conscious, logged choice.

---

## 2. The hard rules

These are non-negotiable. They are what make the method safe.

1. **Sources must be real and checkable.** Every Chinese character and every
   cited scholarly translation must be pulled from a verifiable source at
   translation time and recorded in the worksheet. **Never from model recall.**
   An AI can hallucinate a character or a reading with full confidence. If a
   character or a source cannot be verified, it does not enter the worksheet.

2. **AI never has final authority.** AI gathers, compares, flags, and drafts.
   Adrian decides every line. Nothing ships on AI authority alone.

3. **Evidence before draft — always.** Adrian sees the characters, the
   meaning-range, the scholarly spread, and the flagged forks *before* seeing
   any proposed English. This defeats anchoring (see §4).

4. **Disagreement is recorded, never hidden.** Every line where sources
   diverge is flagged, the fork is shown, and Adrian's choice is logged with a
   one-line reason.

5. **The English is modern; the meaning is faithful.** See Danger 2.

6. **A traceable basis.** Each hexagram's worksheet is kept. The finished deck
   has a documented evidentiary basis a scholar could later check.

---

## 3. Sources — what to triangulate

For each hexagram, assemble (from real, checkable sources):

- **The Chinese source text** — the received text of the Judgment (King Wen)
  and the Image, character by character. Plus, where relevant, the line texts
  (Duke of Zhou layer).
- **Character-level meaning** — for each character, its range of attested
  meanings, ideally including Old Chinese sense where it differs from modern.
- **Multiple scholarly translations / commentaries** — at least three, ideally
  more, spanning different scholarly approaches. Use editions that can be
  legitimately consulted. Public-domain translations (e.g. Legge, 1899) may be
  quoted; in-copyright translations may be *consulted and compared* as research
  but never reproduced or closely paraphrased.

The point of multiple sources is not to copy any of them. It is to see the
*range* — where they converge (high confidence) and where they fork (genuine
ambiguity).

---

## 4. The procedure — one hexagram

### Step 1 — Assemble (AI as gatherer)

AI compiles, into the worksheet, from verified sources only:
- the Chinese text of the Judgment and Image, character by character;
- each character's meaning-range;
- how each consulted scholarly source renders the line.

No drafting yet. Just evidence, with every source recorded.

### Step 2 — Map convergence and divergence (AI as comparer)

AI marks, line by line:
- **Convergent** — points where the sources substantially agree. High
  confidence.
- **Divergent** — points where sources genuinely fork. Each fork is shown:
  reading A (which sources, what it rests on) vs. reading B, and so on.

Still no draft. The output of Step 2 is a clear picture of what is settled and
what is open.

### Step 3 — Adrian forms a first sense (human, before the draft)

Adrian reads the evidence and the forks and forms his own sense of the line —
*before any English draft exists*. This step is mandatory and is the core
anti-anchoring safeguard. Even rough notes are enough; the point is that
Adrian's judgment is seeded by the evidence, not by AI phrasing.

### Step 4 — AI drafts a synthesis (AI as drafter, last)

Now AI proposes a best-meaning rendering of the Judgment and the Image, built
from the Step 1–2 evidence. With the draft, AI states:
- which reading it took at each flagged fork, and why;
- where the draft lands close to an existing translation (expected and fine,
  if it arose from the evidence and is not a copy of distinctive phrasing);
- any line it is genuinely unsure of.

The draft is **one more input**, arriving after Adrian's own sense, not before.

### Step 5 — Adrian verifies and renders the final line (human, decisive)

Adrian compares the draft against his Step 3 sense and the evidence, and
writes the final Judgment and Image. For every flagged fork, Adrian's choice
is logged in the worksheet with a one-line reason. The wording is Adrian's.

### Step 6 — Faithfulness and register check

Against the finished line:
- Meaning faithful to the Bronze Age sense? No modern overlay imported?
- English modern, clear, living? Terse, ancient register preserved (per
  `03_ICHING_GUIDE.md` Layer 2)?
- Read aloud. Does it carry?

### Step 7 — (Optional) expert review

The worksheet is an artifact a sinologist or serious I-Ching scholar could
later check — evidence, forks, decisions, reasons all recorded. Expert review
is designed-in but not a hard dependency. If an expert becomes available, the
contested lines are the priority for their attention.

## 5. The translation worksheet

One per hexagram, kept with the others as the deck's evidentiary record.

```
HEXAGRAM [n] — [Chinese name] / [hexagram name]

--- JUDGMENT ---
Chinese text:        [characters]
Character notes:     [per character: attested meaning-range]
Source renderings:   [source A: "..."]  [source B: "..."]  [source C: "..."]
Convergence:         [what the sources agree on]
Divergence / forks:  [fork 1: reading A (sources) vs reading B (sources) ...]
Adrian's first sense (Step 3, pre-draft):  [notes]
AI synthesis draft (Step 4):               [draft + fork choices + reasons]
FINAL Judgment (Adrian):                   [the rendered line]
Fork decisions logged:  [fork 1: chose A — reason. fork 2: chose B — reason.]

--- IMAGE ---
[same fields as Judgment]

--- CHECKS ---
Meaning faithful, no modern overlay:  [y/n + note]
English modern, register preserved:   [y/n + note]
Read aloud:                            [y/n]
Open questions for possible expert:    [any]
```

---

## 6. On "more accurate than the old translations"

This is achievable, and honestly so — but only in a specific sense. The
rendering can be more accurate because:
- it triangulates *multiple* scholarly readings against the Chinese, rather
  than inheriting one translator's choices wholesale;
- it is in clear modern English, so it miscommunicates less to a present-day
  reader;
- it records its own basis, so its choices are inspectable.

It is **not** more accurate by being more modern in *meaning*, by resolving
ambiguities the scholars left open, or by being produced faster. Accuracy here
comes from rigour and triangulation, not from confidence or fluency. Hold that
distinction. The moment the rendering starts sounding more certain than the
sources warrant, it has drifted from accurate toward merely smooth.

---

## 7. Per-hexagram checklist

- [ ] All characters and sources pulled from verified, checkable sources —
      none from model recall.
- [ ] At least three scholarly renderings triangulated.
- [ ] Convergence and divergence mapped before any draft.
- [ ] Adrian formed a first sense (Step 3) before seeing the AI draft.
- [ ] AI draft arrived last, with fork choices and reasons stated.
- [ ] Every fork decision logged with a one-line reason.
- [ ] Final Judgment and Image written by Adrian.
- [ ] Meaning faithful to the Bronze Age sense — no modern overlay.
- [ ] English modern and clear; terse ancient register preserved.
- [ ] Worksheet kept as the evidentiary record.
- [ ] Read aloud. Adrian wrote and edited the final lines himself.
