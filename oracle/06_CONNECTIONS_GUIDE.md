# 06 — The Connections Guide (Relations)

How to write the connection layer on all 64 cards — the voice that teaches how
a code is kin to the other 63.

Read `CONCEPT.md` and `00_MASTER_WRITING_GUIDE.md` first. The master guide
holds the two-phase frame, the terms, the copyright line, the shared voice
rules. This document covers only what is specific to the **Relations** — the voice
of connection.

Relations is one of the card's voices (CONCEPT.md §4). Like every voice, it
teaches the same code fully, in its own register. Its register is
*relationship*: this code does not stand alone; here is the family it belongs
to, and what it forms with its kin.

---

## 1. Why Relations is a voice, not metadata

The 64 cards are not 64 separate objects. They are a living field. A code is
only half-understood in isolation — its kin are part of what it *is*. So the
connections are not a data footer or an optional basement. They are a genuine
teaching: in a deck that teaches the 64 system, learning how the codes
interlock *is* the system.

Relations also fixes a fault the deck was prone to: connections feeling "bolted
on." They only feel bolted on when shown as machinery — "amino acid: Lysine,
sibling keys [1,14]." Shown as *relationship*, told as story, they belong,
because kinship genuinely deepens the code.

---

## 2. The three kinships

A code is bound to others in three distinct ways. Each is a real, separate
structure; together they make the deck one connected field.

### Codon ring — the family

From the Gene Keys. Each code belongs to one of 22 **codon rings** — a family
of codes bonded through a shared amino acid. A ring has anywhere from one to
six members. Card 1 and Card 14 share the Ring of Fire.

The ring is a *family*: members share a deep theme, and a reader learning one
ring member is helped by meeting its siblings.

### Channel — the partner bond

From Human Design. Each gate forms one or more **channels** — a channel
completes only when its partner gate is also present. 36 channels pair all 64
gates. Card 1 and Card 8 form the Channel of Inspiration.

The channel is a *completion*: two codes that make a whole together, each
bringing what the other lacks.

### Programming partner — the polarity

From the Gene Keys. Each code has one **programming partner** — its polar
complement, the code that sits opposite it and completes its polarity. Card 1
and Card 2: the spark and the compass.

The programming partner is a *polarity*: not a family member and not a channel
completion, but the opposite pole the code is defined against.

These are different pairings. The same card has ring kin, a channel partner,
and a programming partner — usually three *different* cards. That is the
richness: three ways a code reaches out and touches the deck.

---

## 3. The principle — teach the bond, hide the machinery

Every connection is taught as a **relationship told in plain human terms**,
never as technical apparatus.

**Teach (the relationship):**
- *which* card(s) this code is kin to, by name and number;
- *what they share*, or *what they form together*, in plain language;
- *why that kinship matters* to understanding this code.

**Hide (the machinery):**
- the words "codon," "amino acid," the specific amino acid name;
- "stop codon," ring index numbers, circuitry jargon;
- Human Design mechanics — defined/undefined, type, authority, motorized vs.
  awareness streams.

The machinery stays in the build data and the worksheets; it determines *which*
cards are bonded. The reader receives the *motion* — these codes are kin — not
the gears. (This is the same discipline as `05`: no jargon the reader must
decode. And it honours master §6 / CONCEPT.md §9: the lineages may be named —
"a codon ring," "a Human Design channel" — but never leaned on as machinery.)

Naming the structure lightly is fine and is honest teaching: "this code's
codon ring," "the channel it forms." What is forbidden is the apparatus that
needs a framework to parse.

### Name the card, not the number

A related rule, and a common failure: connection prose must **teach the
relationship**, never recite index numbers. "Key 1 shares the Arcana of
Temperance with Key 14" is a failure — it cites numbers and explains nothing.
Write instead what the two cards *are to each other*: "Earth's Breath is the
first spark of the creative fire; Ancestors Bloom is that same fire grown
into harvest — the ring holds the flame at its beginning and its fullness."
Refer to a kin card by its **name**, and teach what the bond *means*. A number
alone is machinery; the relationship is the teaching.

---

## 4. Structure of Relations voice

Relations voice, when opened, teaches the three kinships in this order — from
closest bond to widest family:

**1. The programming partner — the polarity.**
Name the partner card. State the polarity in one or two plain sentences: what
this code and its partner are to each other, the spark and the compass. ~40–70
words.

**2. The channel — the completion.**
Name the partner gate as one of the 64 cards. Name the channel. Teach, in
plain relationship terms, what the two codes *form together* — what each
brings, what completes when they join. ~50–90 words.

**3. The codon ring — the family.**
Name the ring. Name its sibling cards. Teach the shared theme of the family —
what these codes have in common, the energy the ring carries across all its
members. If the ring has many members, the teaching is of the *family theme*,
not a roll-call. ~50–90 words.

Lengths are typical ranges, not gates (CONCEPT.md §3). A bond teaches at
whatever length genuine understanding needs. If a ring or channel relationship
is genuinely simple, it is taught briefly; if it is rich, it is given room.

Each kinship names the related cards so the reader can travel to them — the
Relations is also the deck's navigation between cards.

## 5. The Tarot within Relations

The Tarot Major Arcana enters at the **ring** level — each codon ring carries
one Arcana as its archetypal face (CONCEPT.md §2). So the Tarot is taught
*inside* the ring kinship, not as a separate voice:

When teaching the codon ring (§4, part 3), name the ring's Arcana as a single
evocative resonance — "the Ring of Fire carries Temperance" — one image, never
explained or defended. The Arcana gives the ring family a recognizable face; a
reader who knows Tarot feels a whole network light up, a reader who does not
receives a quietly evocative word. Either way it costs nothing.

The ring-to-Arcana assignment is not standardized doctrine — no official
mapping exists (it is the maker's own contemplative correspondence). Treat it
as authored, not sourced. See `CONCEPT.md` §9 on lineage.

---

## 6. What is doctrine and what is authored

Keep these straight — they have opposite handling.

- **Ring membership** (which codes are bonded in a ring) — Gene Keys doctrine.
  It has a right answer. The deck's data is treated as the working source;
  flagged for verification against Gene Keys source material.
- **Channel pairings** (which gates form a channel) — Human Design doctrine.
  Right answer. The synthesis data carries this; verifiable against Human
  Design sources if needed.
- **Programming partners** — Gene Keys doctrine. Right answer.
- **The ring-to-Tarot assignment** — *not* doctrine. Authored by contemplation.
- **The prose of every connection** — entirely the writer's own. Teach the
  relationship in original words; never reproduce a source's phrasing.

## 7. The deeper correlation layer

Beyond the three kinships, the deck carries a **deeper correlation layer**
(CONCEPT.md §8) — optional study depth showing how the systems interlock at a
finer grain. It is *not central* and not a primary voice; it is offered to the
seeker who wants to go further, never imposed on the seeker who wants a
reading. It is governed here, with Relations, because it is connective material.

The layer has three parts. The vault holds source material for each.

### 7.1 The eight trigrams

Every hexagram is two trigrams (Heaven, Earth, Fire, Water, Thunder, Wind,
Mountain, Lake). The trigrams are the building blocks of the 64 and carry
their own elemental natures. In the correlation layer, the trigram teaching
shows *what the code is made of* — its two component forces and what they do
when combined. Taught plainly, as the structural anatomy of the hexagram.

### 7.2 The Eight Immortals

The eight Daoist immortals, each correlated with a trigram. They give the
trigram forces a human, mythic face — the way the Tarot Arcana gives a ring
family a face. Taught as resonance and story, never as doctrine to defend.

### 7.3 The Tarot's Golden Dawn correspondences

The astrological and Kabbalistic depth *of the Tarot voice* — not a separate
system. Every Tarot card carries a traditional Golden Dawn correspondence
scheme: a zodiac sign, planet, or element; a Hebrew letter and its meaning; a
Golden Dawn attribution. When a seeker goes deep into a ring's Arcana, this is
the fine grain they meet beneath it.

Handling:
- These correspondences are *attributes of the Tarot*. They belong with the
  ring's Arcana (§5), as its deeper layer — not given independent billing.
- Name them plainly, as a quiet resonance, when the seeker has descended that
  far. Never as a fifth voice, never on the card's first reading.
- The scheme comes from one source document and follows the Golden Dawn /
  Thoth tradition. Treat it as *sourced reference* requiring verification, not
  as the maker's own contemplation — and credit the lineage (CONCEPT.md §10).

### Principle for the whole layer

The correlation layer is **expandable** — further correspondences may be added
over time. Whatever is added obeys the same rule as Relations: teach the
*connection* as plain relationship or resonance; keep the machinery (technical
attributions, index numbers, jargon) out of the reader's face. It deepens the
connective tissue; it never becomes a primary voice.

---

## 8. The whole-system map

Beyond the per-card Relations voice, the deck has a **map** — a whole-system view
where all 64 cards and all three kinship fields are seen at once (CONCEPT.md §7).
The card teaches the *local* connection; the map teaches the *global*
structure. A seeker learning the system needs, at some point, to see the whole.

The map is a design artifact, specified separately, not governed by this
writing guide. This guide governs the connection *writing* on each card; the
map governs how the connections are *seen*.
