# 01 — The Description Spec

How to write the `gene_keys.description` field on all 64 cards.

Read `00_MASTER_WRITING_GUIDE.md` first. The master guide holds the two-phase
frame (§1), the terms (§3), the card-coherence doctrine (§5), the copyright
line (§6), the shared voice rules (§7) and the human-writing discipline — all
of which apply here and are not repeated. This document covers only what is
specific to the **description**: the Shadow / Gift / Siddhi prose that teaches
the reader the code.

The description **teaches** — the reader understands the code. (Its companion,
the invocation, *transmits* — see `02`.) Two different organs of the card. Per
master §5.1, the description's unique job is the three frequencies; it does not
re-explain the hexagram or the natural image, which belong to the I-Ching
section.

---

## 1. The flow — what the section does to a reader

A description is a descent and a return. The reader arrives carrying the
Shadow (everyone enters a code at the Shadow frequency). The prose walks them
through:

1. **Recognition** — they see themselves in the Shadow, without shame.
2. **Diagnosis** — the Shadow has two faces, repressed and reactive. They
   locate which one is theirs.
3. **The turn** — the Gift is not the Shadow's opposite. It is the *same
   energy*, no longer afraid. This is the hinge.
4. **The horizon** — the Siddhi is a state, not a goal. It should feel
   slightly beyond reach. That is correct.

Arc: **contraction → diagnosis → release → transcendence.** Every card walks
the same path; only the terrain changes.

---

## 2. Canonical structure

Opening line, then three paragraphs. The *structure* is fixed for every card,
no exceptions. The *lengths* below are typical ranges, not gates — see the note
at the end of this section.

### Opening line — one sentence
A threshold statement. Names the *core tension* of this code in one breath —
not the Shadow, not the Gift, but the *field* the whole code lives in. Reads
almost like a proverb. It is the card's hook and the reader's first foothold.
Typically around 15 to 22 words.

### Shadow paragraph
- Open by naming the Shadow and the *felt experience* of living there. What it
  is actually like. Concrete, not abstract.
- Give the **repressed** form (collapses inward — passivity, absence, numbness,
  denial) and the **reactive** form (explodes outward — aggression, control,
  grasping, frenzy). One clear clause each. The reader must be able to find
  themselves in exactly one. Write them as two different people.
- Close on the underlying *fear or belief* driving the whole pattern. The root.
- Typically around 70 to 90 words.

### Gift paragraph
- Open with the turn: state plainly that the Gift is the *same energy* as the
  Shadow, metabolized — not its opposite, not its replacement.
- Show the Gift in a life: concrete, embodied, present tense. A person, doing
  something, in the world. Not a definition of the Gift — a sighting of it.
- Close on what becomes possible from here.
- Typically around 70 to 90 words.

### Siddhi paragraph
- Slightly shorter than the other two. It should feel like air thinning at
  altitude.
- Describe the Siddhi as a *state*, present tense, spacious. Never as an
  achievement or a destination.
- End on an image, not an explanation. The reader closes on a held breath.
- Typically around 60 to 80 words.

### On the length figures

A description usually lands near 230 words total. The figures above are
*typical ranges, not gates*. Their purpose is consistency — so the 64 cards
sit evenly together rather than ranging wildly — and a quick check, not a
rule that overrides the writing. If a card genuinely wants 300 words or holds
its truth in 190, that is allowed. But ask why first: far outside the range is
usually a sign of padding a thin card or cramming a full one, not of a card
that truly needs the space. Let the figure flag the question; let the writing
answer it.

## 3. Description-specific voice notes

The shared voice rules in master guide §7 all apply. Two notes specific to the
description:

- **The "not X, it is Y" construction belongs here, once** — as the Gift
  hinge, where you state the Gift is the same energy as the Shadow. That is its
  single best use. Do not also use it elsewhere in the same card.
- **Repressed and reactive must read as two distinct people**, not two clauses
  you filled in. Picture two actual humans when you write them. That keeps the
  diagnosis alive instead of schematic.

## 4. Worked example — Card 2, "Beyond the Shell"

Shadow: Dislocation · Gift: Orientation · Siddhi: Unity

This example is ship-ready: it obeys every rule, including no em dashes. Use
it as the standard, not as a draft to be cleaned up.

> Direction is not something you choose once. It is something the body keeps
> finding, the way water keeps finding down.
>
> Dislocation is the quiet certainty that you are in the wrong place and the
> right place does not exist. It does not announce itself. It shows up as a
> decision you keep not making, a room you cannot settle in, a life lived
> slightly to the side of itself. Repressed, it goes still. You stop looking,
> call the drift acceptance, and wait. Reactive, it builds scaffolding: rigid
> plans, agendas, a five-year structure thrown up against the groundlessness.
> Underneath both is the belief that orientation must come from outside you.
>
> Orientation is that same restlessness, finally trusted. It is not a direction
> chosen by the mind. It is the body turning, the way a plant turns, before any
> reason has been given. A person living here stops interrogating the path and
> starts feeling for it, and the path, oddly, starts to firm up underfoot.
> What looked like being lost was the sense organ working. From here you can
> move without a map and still arrive.
>
> Unity is what remains when there is no longer a self that can be in the wrong
> place. Nothing to orient, because nothing is separate from the field it moves
> in. Not the relief of arriving somewhere. The end of the distance itself.

About 235 words. The repressed/reactive beats use a period and a colon, not em
dashes. The "It is not X. It is Y" turn appears once, in the Gift paragraph,
as the hinge — its single best use.

## 5. Per-description checklist

- [ ] Opening line names the field, proverb-like, ~15–22 words.
- [ ] Shadow paragraph has both repressed and reactive, one clause each,
      written as two distinct people.
- [ ] Shadow paragraph ends on the root fear/belief.
- [ ] Gift paragraph opens by naming it as the *same energy* metabolized.
- [ ] Gift paragraph shows a person in the world, not a definition.
- [ ] Siddhi paragraph is shorter, present tense, ends on an image.
- [ ] Total length near the typical range (~230 words); if far outside, the
      reason is the writing, not padding or cramming.
- [ ] Does only the description's job (master §5.1) — no re-explaining the
      hexagram or the natural image.
- [ ] Master guide §6 (copyright) and §7 (voice) obeyed — no source naming,
      no unverified flourishes, no em dashes, no triads.
- [ ] Reads as observed, not defined; carries lived particularity (master §7).
- [ ] Card status set (master §10): `scaffold` if AI-drafted, `final` once
      Adrian has personally written or reworked it.
