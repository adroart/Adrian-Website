# Universal Language — Marketing & Positioning

The strategic spine of the Universal Language project. Captures the mission,
the product structure, pricing, the network logic, and the language rules
that protect the work from drifting into ecommerce-coded patterns. Written
as a living reference. Revisit and revise as the project evolves.

**Status:** v1, drafted 2026-05-22 from a multi-hour strategic conversation
between Adrian and Claude. Anchored to that conversation; reflects decisions
made there. Open questions are flagged inline.

---

## I. The mission

> **The Universal Language is a coordination language for consciousness.**

The 64 codes are a shared symbolic system that integrates I Ching, Gene
Keys, Human Design, and Tarot into a single accessible vocabulary. The
deck teaches it. The sculptures embody it. The network connects the
people who carry it.

This is not "another oracle deck." It is an artwork-as-system: an
artistic practice whose purpose is to give spiritually-attuned people
a real way to find, recognize, and coordinate with each other through
their consciousness signatures. The art is the anchor. The system is
the language. The network is what the language makes possible.

### The principle that protects everything else

> **The wisdom is free. The objects are art.**

The Universal Language website is the free, open primary surface for the
work. Anyone can read the 64 codes, do a reading, sit with the invocations,
and explore the system without paying anything. The website is not a
paywalled tier; it is the welcome mat.

Revenue comes from **objects that embody the codes** — the decks (as
practice tools and collectibles) and the sculptures (as art and as
placed network nodes). The wisdom does not require a paywall. The
artistry does.

This principle is a positioning statement against the broader oracle and
wellness market, much of which monetizes spiritual content directly. The
Universal Language refuses that pattern.

## II. The artifact — how the work is made

**Honest production credit.** The Universal Language sculptures are
laser-cut wood pieces, designed by Adrian Rasmussen and finished by
skilled airbrush artisans in Indonesia under the artist's direction.
Every piece is individually airbrushed; no two finishes are identical.
This is the bronze-casting tradition adapted to wood: editioned form,
individually hand-finished surface, the artist's eye on every piece,
the artisans' hand on every finish.

**This honesty is non-negotiable.** Marketing copy must not claim
"hand-painted by the artist" when the airbrush work is done by the
artist's team. The correct phrasing is:
- "Hand-finished by skilled airbrush artisans under the artist's
  direction"
- "Individually airbrushed by Adrian's team"
- "Each piece individually finished — no two are alike"

What this language does well: it dignifies the artisans' labor, it
accurately describes the production, and it sits comfortably in the
bronze-sculpture / multiples tradition. Hirst doesn't paint his spots.
Koons doesn't make his sculptures. McQueen didn't sew his dresses.
Collaborative production is normal and respectable in serious art
practice. What damages credibility is misrepresenting it.

## III. The product ladder

Six tiers, from free to commission. Each serves a different audience and
a different commitment level. Together they form a coherent collector
funnel from $0 to $8K+.

| Tier | Price | Edition | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free website | $0 | open | Welcome mat — read codes, do readings, explore the system |
| Pearl registration | $0 | open | Register your personal code, become discoverable on the network |
| Normal Oracle Deck | $30 | open edition | Practice tool, giftable, unlocks verified carrier status |
| Premium Oracle Deck | $111 | open edition | Deluxe version: signed, wood box with integrated art |
| Small editioned sculpture | $295-350 | edition of 50 | Network node, first sculpture tier, includes normal deck |
| Medium editioned sculpture | $1,111+ | edition of 10 | Studio collector, includes normal deck |
| Large editioned sculpture | $2,500-3,200 | edition of 5 | Principal work, includes premium deck |
| Custom commission | $8K+ start | unique, by inquiry | Bespoke variation, includes premium deck + personal artist gesture |

### What's deliberately NOT in this ladder

- **No prints.** The deck handles accessibility; prints would create a
  redundant tier that dilutes both. Skip.
- **No unstained / cheaper-node tier yet.** Defer until the network is
  visibly real (10-20 placed nodes minimum). Revisit only if needed.
- **No second editions yet.** Each edition is the edition. Selling-out
  events are real and final until further notice.
- **No subscription model.** The work is not Software-as-a-Service.
  Don't paywall recurring access.
- **No NFTs or crypto components.** The work is physical-and-network,
  not digital scarcity.
- **No tiered membership badges (bronze/silver/gold).** The community
  is real, not gamified.

## IV. The configurator pricing model

Each sculpture size has a **base price** and **optional configuration
add-ons**. Configurations are artistic choices, not sales tactics. Each
add-on carries real material and labor cost.

### Small editioned sculpture
- **Base configuration** — $295
- **With crystal frame** — $350 (+$55)
  Inset semi-precious stones, matched to the code's color signature.
- **(Potential future tier)** Charged crystal frame — $395-425
  Same as crystal frame, but stones are charged/cleansed by the artist
  with a small ritual, accompanied by a card describing the ceremony.
  Only do this if it's truly part of the practice.

### Medium editioned sculpture (58cm)
- **Base configuration** — $1,111
- Goes up with add-ons (specific configurations TBD)

### Large editioned sculpture (90cm)
- **Base configuration** — $2,500
- **With integrated lighting** — $3,200 (+$700)
  LED system embedded behind the laser-cut form, casting the code's
  pattern onto the wall in shadow. For installation in dimmed spaces.

### Custom commission
- By inquiry only. **Never display commission prices on the site.**
- Pricing is conversational, project-by-project.
- Base commission for ~1.5-2m scaled large: ~$8K-12K start.
- Architectural-scale (2-3m): $20K-40K and up.
- Each commission is a unique work, signed and certified as unique,
  outside the edition system. No quantity cap on commissions — they
  are not part of the editioned work.

### How to present configurators

**Don't:** "Starting at $295! Add crystals for $55! Add lighting for
$700!" — this reads as upselling.

**Do:** Present the base, present the add-on as a configuration choice
with its own descriptive paragraph explaining what it does artistically.
Let the buyer toggle. The total updates. The buyer chooses how the work
behaves in their space.

The framing is: *artistic configuration*, not *purchase upgrade*.

## V. The deck-with-sculpture principle

**The deck and the sculpture are designed to work together.**

A sculpture alone becomes furniture. A deck alone becomes occasional
practice. Together they form a living artwork in the home: the sculpture
grounds the deck's daily practice; the deck animates the sculpture's
presence.

The deck is included with every sculpture acquisition because the
sculpture is incomplete without it. The two are not a bundle. They are
**one work in two forms**.

### What's included with each tier

- **Small sculpture** ($295-350) includes the **normal oracle deck**
- **Medium sculpture** ($1,111+) includes the **normal oracle deck**
- **Large sculpture** ($2,500-3,200) includes the **premium oracle deck**
  (signed, numbered, wood box)
- **Custom commission** ($8K+) includes the **premium oracle deck** plus
  a personal artist gesture (handwritten letter, signed drawing, or
  similar — never a templated extra)

### Why this isn't a discount

Don't frame the included deck as "free" or "bonus" or "10% off." The
correct framing is:

> *"The Universal Language deck arrives with every sculpture
> acquisition. The sculpture is the embodiment of a code; the deck is
> the means of returning to it. Together they form the daily practice
> the work was designed to support."*

The deck is product-design, not sales-design. The sculpture-without-deck
is an incomplete version of what's being acquired.

### The social dimension of the bundle

A sculpture is a private possession. The deck makes the sculpture
**social**: visitors who pull a card see the sculpture as the
embodiment of what they just drew. The work becomes a participatory
artifact in the home. Each home with both becomes a small node where
others encounter the system.

This is part of why the bundle works. The deck is the entry tool that
lets *anyone* engage with the work — including the friends and family
of the sculpture owner.

## VI. The deck strategy

The deck does **triple duty**: practice tool, gift object, viral
distribution engine.

### Normal Oracle Deck — $30

- Open edition, no numbering
- Printed at the highest quality production allows
- Beautifully presented: physical packaging worthy of being unwrapped
  as a gift without re-packaging
- Includes a printed guidebook in the artist's voice
- Designed to be **giftable by default**: any deck can be sent to a
  friend without re-wrapping
- **Cross-references the free website** for deeper readings of each code

### Premium Oracle Deck — $111

- **Open edition** (no edition cap) — the premium deck is the deluxe
  version of the work, not a numbered collectible. It can be produced
  as long as the work continues.
- **Wood box with integrated art** — the box itself carries artwork
  from the project; it's a presentation object collectors keep on a
  shelf
- **Hand-signed by the artist** — each deck signed personally
- Slightly nicer card stock and printed guidebook
- Differentiated from the normal deck by **what's in the box** (the
  wood box, the signing, the art), not by edition scarcity

### Why $111 (and why no edition cap)

The price echoes the medium sculpture at $1,111 — both use the
repeating-ones motif, creating a quiet harmonic across the price ladder.
That harmonic is intentional and part of the brand's typographic
identity.

The open-edition decision is deliberate: the premium deck is a
**deluxe production version**, not a closed art-edition. This allows
the artist to keep producing the premium deck indefinitely while
continuing to serve serious buyers who want the boxed-and-signed
version. Many serious artists run their trade book alongside a
deluxe edition this way (Hirst's Visual Candy with multiple editions
including signed, for example). The signing and the wood box are what
make it special — not artificial scarcity.

### Why these specific prices

Most oracle decks sit at $30-60 from emerging-to-indie publishers.
$30 is at the accessible end — clean entry-level pricing that gets
the deck into many hands. The premium at $111 is a meaningful 3.7x
premium that signals "this is the deluxe version" without being
aspirational about market position. The price never rises above this
range until reputation supports it.

### The "give a code" feature (deferred / future)

Architecturally worth supporting from day one: owners of the deck (or
anyone) can pull a card on the website and send it as a gift to a
friend — as a digital reading via email, or as a physical card mailed
directly, or as a deck with the chosen card marked as "your card from
[friend]." This turns the deck/website ecosystem into a gifting
platform. Don't build day one. Architect for it.

## VII. The pricing philosophy

### Prices appear in the Acquire section, not on buttons

The button announces what's being offered. The detail section delivers
the price, the edition count, the remaining availability, and the
configuration choices. This sequencing is important:

- **Button:** *"Acquire / the Original Hand-Finished Sculpture / Editions in three sizes"*
- **Detail page:** Full per-size breakdown with prices, edition counts, configurations

This protects the deck's dignity (no price-on-button) while serving
conversion (price visible inside, where it builds trust).

### When to show edition counts

- **Editions ≤ 12:** edition count is a strong collector signal, show it
- **Editions 13-25:** show inside detail page, not on button
- **Editions 26+:** don't use the count as a scarcity signal — use
  framing instead ("Edition of 50, each individually finished")

The small edition of 50 is **rescued from anti-scarcity reading** by the
network framing: 50 isn't "we made too many," it's "the network needs 50
nodes." Quantity becomes constitutive of the work, not arbitrary inventory.

### The principle for raising prices later

As editions sell through, prices may lift on remaining stock and on
future works. **Never lower an edition price**, and never raise it
mid-edition without good reason. Collectors who paid earlier shouldn't
feel devalued by later buyers paying less; collectors who paid earlier
should feel rewarded by later buyers paying more.

The large in particular has headroom: current $2,500 base could rise to
$2,800-3,000 as the edition sells through, and substantially higher for
future editions or works.

## VIII. The network — what it actually is

### The map

A real, public, interactive map showing:

- **Placed sculptures** — small, medium, large — with their owners
  (with permission), locations, dates placed, photographs of the piece
  in its placed home, dedications/notes from the owner
- **Registered carriers** — people who own decks and have registered
  their personal Pearl/code, with their location (city level), code,
  bio, what they offer, and contact preference
- **Founding nodes** — the first holder of each code gets permanent
  acknowledgment as that code's founding node
- **Filterable** by code, location, type (sculpture vs carrier),
  availability, skills

### The three participation layers

1. **Registered carriers** (free) — anyone can register their personal
   code, become discoverable on the map. No purchase required beyond
   (optional) deck or sculpture. This is the spiritual-LinkedIn entry.

2. **Verified carriers** — deck owners (normal or premium) get a
   verified status badge after deck purchase. Signals genuine
   investment in the work without paywalling the community.

3. **Physical anchors** — sculpture owners. Their pieces are placed
   on the map alongside their carrier profile. They are the founding
   nodes of the physical network.

**No tier paywalls the community.** Higher tiers pay for the physical
artifact (deck, sculpture), not for access to the network. The wisdom
is free. The objects are art. The network is the bridge between them.

### The "spiritual LinkedIn" concept

The 64 codes function as a coordination language. People can find each
other by:

- Code (find everyone who carries Code 14)
- Code pairing (find people whose code structurally complements yours —
  programming partners, codon ring siblings)
- Location (find aligned people in a city you're visiting)
- Skills / offerings (find a healer, designer, coach, teacher who carries
  a specific code)

This turns the map into **functional infrastructure** — people use it for
real purposes: collaboration, friendship, professional pairing, romance,
travel, community building.

This is what makes the network meaningful beyond "dots on a map." It
becomes a way for spiritually-attuned people to find each other through
a shared symbolic language.

### What the network is NOT

- Not a Discord server (don't build one)
- Not a tier-badge gamification system
- Not a paywalled community
- Not an MLM-style ambassador program
- Not a daily-engagement chat platform
- Not a content-feed social network

The network is a **discovery directory** with an annual in-person
gathering and a permanent map. It is real, not performative. It refuses
the engagement-maximizing patterns of mainstream social platforms.

## IX. The community phasing

Realistic about what to build when. Avoiding the common artist-network
trap of over-building before users exist.

### Phase 1 (now → 6 months): Foundation

- Map of placed sculptures with full node-holder profiles
- Pearl registration system (calculator → personal code → profile)
- Basic public directory: name, location, code, bio, contact
- Founding-node acknowledgments for first buyers
- Sculpture acquisition includes deck + map registration + welcome

**This alone is more than 99% of artist projects offer.** Don't move
to Phase 2 until Phase 1 is solid and used by real people.

### Phase 2 (6-18 months): Discovery deepens

- Search by skills/offerings
- Pairing logic (system shows complementary codes)
- Direct messaging or contact-request system within the platform
- The first annual in-person gathering

### Phase 3 (18-36 months): Infrastructure

- Tens of thousands of registered carriers
- Apprenticeship / reader training tier — node-holders trained to give
  readings in the artist's tradition
- Featured listings for practitioners (potential revenue stream)
- Code-specific gatherings (50 holders of Code 14 meeting in a city)

### Phase 4 (3-5 years): Full expression

- The Universal Language is a real cultural object
- The first edition book documents the network's first phase
- People know their Pearl the way they know their astro sign
- The mission is visibly working

### The patience principle

Networks that matter accrete; they aren't manufactured. The first 10
nodes feel sparse. The first 100 have shape. The first 1,000 are a real
artifact. Build the map well, sell the first batch with care, host one
good gathering. **Let the community emerge from real use, not from
elaborate platform features built speculatively.**

The mistake to avoid: spending two years building platform features
while neglecting the sculptures and decks. The platform serves the
work; it doesn't replace it.

## X. The annual gathering

One real, in-person, multi-day event per year. Not a Zoom call.

- 30-50 attendees (small enough to be intimate, large enough to be real)
- Location: Bali, or a different significant place each year (a
  node-holder's region, a retreat center)
- Two or three days
- Code placement ceremony for new pieces
- Discussions about the system, the codes, the network
- Meals cooked together
- A reading by the artist
- Documented (with attendees' permission) for those who couldn't come

### Why this matters

The annual gathering is what makes the network **real** rather than
rhetorical. Once a year, the dots on the map become humans in a room.
The people who couldn't come hear about it. The work's reality is
reinforced.

### Pricing

Cover costs. Don't profit-maximize. A 3-day Bali gathering with food
and accommodation could be $500-1,500 per person, depending on what's
included. Some node-holders attend every year; some never. Both are
fine. The gathering exists; attending is optional.

### Operational honesty

Hosting one good event per year is significant work — typically 2-3
months of planning. **Be honest with yourself about whether this is
sustainable.** If hosting isn't realistic, partner with someone who can
help run it. Don't promise a gathering you can't deliver.

## XI. The pilgrimage layer

A low-cost, high-meaning feature: as the network of placed sculptures
grows, node-holders traveling can connect with other node-holders
(mutually-permitted on the map) and visit each other's placed pieces.

The map becomes a **geography of meaningful destinations**. People
visiting a city can see which codes are placed there, who holds them,
whether they'd welcome a visit.

This requires no infrastructure beyond the map and a permission system.
Just enable it from day one. Let it happen.

## XII. Marketing language — rules and patterns

### Never use

- "Buy Now" / "Add to Cart" (use "Acquire")
- "Limited time" / countdown timers
- "Only X left!" scarcity nudges (other than honest "N of 50 placed")
- "Don't miss out" / urgency framing
- Social-proof toasts ("Sarah from Berlin just bought…")
- "Sale" / discount badges / strikethrough prices
- "Free shipping" as a marketing claim — bake it in silently
- "Hand-painted by the artist" when the work is finished by artisans
- "Spiritual / mystical / mystic" overused as a category tag
- Generic wellness language ("manifest," "high-vibe," "good vibes only")

### Always use

- **"Acquire"** — the verb
- **"The original"** — what's being acquired
- **"Edition of N"** — when N ≤ 12
- **"Individually hand-finished"** / **"Individually airbrushed"** —
  honest production credit
- **"Available in three sizes"** — when introducing the size structure
- **"Place a code on the map"** — for the small edition's network role
- **"For commissioned variations, write to the studio"** — the only
  acceptable framing for custom inquiry
- **"The wisdom is free; the objects are art"** — the foundational
  principle, can appear in About pages

### The tone

Restrained. Art-world. The deck doesn't pitch — it presents. Every
piece of copy should feel like a museum plate or a gallery's wall
text, not a checkout page.

When in doubt, ask: *"Would this language fit on a museum plate?"* If
yes, ship it. If no, rewrite.

## XIII. The acquire-section design principles

The Acquire section is where conversion happens. It must be designed
with the care of a gallery page, not a shop checkout.

### Required elements

1. **The piece in context** — photographs of the sculpture in real
   spaces, not isolated against white. Buyers need to see it living
   somewhere.
2. **Honest production description** — laser-cut, hand-finished, by
   whom, where, how
3. **The three sizes** — clearly differentiated, each with its own
   role in the work (entry / studio / principal)
4. **Per-size configurations** — base + optional add-ons, with
   descriptive prose for each add-on
5. **Edition counts** — per size, with remaining availability
6. **The deck-included note** — visible, clearly framed as part of
   the work
7. **The network registration note** — what happens after acquisition
   (the map, the carrier profile, the welcome)
8. **What it's not** — gentle but clear: this is editioned art, not a
   print run; this is a real wood object, not digital; this is for
   collectors who want to live with the work
9. **Inquiry path** — for commissions, large variations, custom needs

### Required absences

- No countdown timers
- No "X people viewing"
- No discount codes prominent
- No upsell modals
- No abandoned-cart emails (or if they exist, gentle and infrequent)

## XIV. The collector funnel

How a buyer typically moves through the tiers:

1. **Discovery** — finds the free website (search, social, referral)
2. **Engagement** — reads a few codes, does a reading, learns the
   system
3. **Pearl registration** — registers their personal code, becomes
   visible on the map (free, frictionless)
4. **Deck purchase** — $30 normal deck, begins daily practice. Verified
   carrier status. Often gifts a second deck to a friend within a
   few months.
5. **First sculpture** — small at $295-350. Their code anchored in
   their home + on the map. Now a physical anchor as well as a
   carrier.
6. **Deepening** — medium at $1,111+. Or a second small in a different
   code that calls them.
7. **Large** — $2,500+. The principal work, with premium deck included.
8. **Commission** — for collectors who want bespoke at architectural
   scale.

**Not everyone goes the full distance, and that's fine.** A buyer who
stops at the deck and stays as a verified carrier is contributing to
the network. A buyer who never registers and only owns a deck still
matters. The funnel is a typical path, not a required one.

### The friend-of-collector path

A separate funnel:
1. Friend of a sculpture-owner visits, sees the work
2. The owner pulls a card from their deck for the friend
3. The friend has a moment of recognition with their drawn code
4. They go to the free website, read more
5. They register their Pearl
6. They buy a deck for themselves
7. They eventually buy a sculpture

This funnel is powered entirely by the deck-with-sculpture pairing.
Without the deck in the home, the sculpture stays private; visitors
admire it but don't engage with the system. With the deck, every
visitor becomes a potential entry point.

## XV. Strategic principles — the work's commitments

These are the principles that protect the work from drifting into
patterns that would damage it. When facing a tactical decision, return
to these:

1. **The wisdom is free; the objects are art.** Never paywall the
   readings.

2. **Honesty over aspiration.** Describe production accurately. Set
   prices that fit market position, not aspirational position.

3. **The artisans' labor is dignified.** Always credit. Never claim
   "hand-painted by the artist" when artisans do the finish work.

4. **Restraint over noise.** Refuse scarcity timers, discount badges,
   urgency language. The work doesn't beg.

5. **Real community over performed community.** The network is a
   directory + an annual gathering + a map, not a Discord server or
   a tier-badge system.

6. **Patience over platform-first thinking.** The work comes first; the
   platform serves the work. Don't build features for users who don't
   exist yet.

7. **Bundles as product design, not sales tactics.** The deck arrives
   with every sculpture because the sculpture is incomplete without
   it, not as a discount or bonus.

8. **The network's value is in the people, not the features.** Build
   the minimum infrastructure that lets real people find real people.
   The rest emerges.

9. **The art is the contribution.** No tacked-on charity component.
   The work's purpose is the work — and the network it makes possible.

10. **Refuse the engagement-maximizing patterns of mainstream platforms.**
    The Universal Language is anti-doomscroll. It is the opposite of
    feeds. It is a quiet, slow, deep relationship to a system.

## XVI. Open strategic questions

Honest about what's unresolved. These get revisited as the project
evolves and as real data comes in.

### Painted vs unpainted finish

Existing pieces show that unpainted sculptures sell faster than
painted at current price points. Open question whether to offer both
finishes within the same edition pool (different prices, same edition
of 50 total). Decision deferred until more market data.

### The unstained-tier question

Whether to introduce a stained-only small edition at a lower price
(~$150-180) once the network is visibly real. Currently deferred —
the dignity work at the small tier requires the artist's hand
(airbrush), and removing it dilutes the edition. Revisit after first
network milestones.

### Premium deck edition size

Currently planned as 333 or 444. Decision pending — depends on
production cost and confidence in initial sell-through.

### Commission cap

Whether to formally cap commissions per code (e.g., max 3 per code) or
leave commissions completely open. Currently leaning open. Revisit if
commission demand becomes unmanageable.

### Annual gathering location

Bali default for the first gathering. Future gatherings may rotate to
significant node-locations. Decision per-year.

### Reader / apprenticeship structure

How to train and certify readers who can give readings in the tradition.
Significant scope; defer to Phase 3 (18-36 months out). Open question
of what certification looks like, what the training curriculum is, how
the artist maintains quality control.

### The book

Five-year horizon. What it documents, what format, who publishes,
whether it's a Phaidon/Taschen-style art monograph or a smaller
self-published artifact. Decision deferred.

## XVII. The artist's posture

How Adrian shows up in relation to the work and the community:

- **As maker, not influencer.** The work is the primary output. Social
  media exists in service of the work, not the other way around.
- **As host, not guru.** The annual gathering is hosted, not led.
  Adrian is a fellow node-holder who happens to have made the system.
- **As writer, not preacher.** The codes' meanings are written from
  inside the practice, not from above it. Adrian writes about the codes
  as someone living with them, not as an authority dispensing wisdom.
- **As artist, not entrepreneur.** Pricing decisions, community
  decisions, platform decisions all run through the question: *does
  this serve the work?* Not: *does this maximize revenue?*

This posture is what protects the work from becoming a "spiritual brand"
or a "wellness business." The work is art. The artist is an artist.
Everything else serves that.
