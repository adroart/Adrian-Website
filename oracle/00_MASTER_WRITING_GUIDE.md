# Universal Language Oracle — Master Writing Guide

The document you open first. It governs the writing of all 64 cards: what
every written part is, the order they come in, the shared rules they all obey,
and the workflow for taking one card from blank to finished.

Five companion documents go deeper on the harder parts:
- `01_DESCRIPTION_SPEC.md` — the Gene Keys description (what the reader understands)
- `02_INVOCATION_GUIDE.md` — the whole-card invocation (what the reader embodies)
- `03_ICHING_GUIDE.md` — the layered I-Ching section (lineage made reachable)
- `04_TRANSLATION_METHOD.md` — how the classic I-Ching text is freshly rendered
- `05_KEYWORDS_GUIDE.md` — the keyword layer (the at-a-glance pinpoint)

These documents exist so that the 64 cards come out as one coherent body of
work, in one voice, legally clean, and unmistakably human.

---

## 1. The two phases — who is writing, and when

The deck is built in two phases. Every document in this set must be read with
the current phase in mind, because the standard is the same but the writer is
not.

**Phase 1 — the scaffold.** AI fills all 64 cards with the fullest, most
faithful, legally clean text it can produce, following these guides. This is a
complete, internally coherent draft — real substance, not placeholders. It is
**not** the major release. Its job is to give Adrian 64 solid, consistent
cards to work *from*, and to carry as much true meaning as possible without
copyright exposure.

**Phase 2 — the art project.** Adrian goes through every card personally, over
time, refining and replacing until each card is genuinely his. The major
release is when this is done. Phase 2 is the real work; Phase 1 is its
foundation.

**Two rules that protect this:**

- *The scaffold is consciously provisional.* Phase 1 text should be good — but
  it must never pretend to be the finished art. The danger is seduction: if
  the scaffold is decent, the pull to leave a card untouched is real, and
  Phase 2 quietly never finishes. Every card carries a status (see §8) so
  scaffold and finished work are never confused.
- *The standard does not change between phases.* The copyright line, the voice
  rules, the structure — all of it binds Phase 1 AI work exactly as it binds
  Phase 2. The scaffold is held to the same bar; it is simply known to be
  replaceable.

When a passage in these guides says "write from what you have seen" or "Adrian
decides," that is the Phase 2 standard and the ideal. In Phase 1, AI writes the
most faithful, careful version it can toward that ideal, knowing Adrian will
later make it true.

## 2. What the deck is

64 cards. Each card is a single **code** — one unit of meaning — mapped across
four lineages at once:

- **I-Ching** — the hexagram, its two trigrams, the classic text
- **Gene Keys** — the Shadow, the Gift, the Siddhi (three frequencies)
- **Human Design** — the gate, its center in the body, its keyword
- **Tarot** — the Major Arcana, carried at the codon-ring level

The four lineages are not four topics. They are **four views of one thing**.
For any given card, the hexagram, the gate, the Gift and the Arcana all point
at the *same underlying energy* from different angles. The deck's job, and
especially the invocation's job, is to speak that one energy, not to tour the
four systems.

The deck is published as both a fine-art series and an interactive oracle.
The writing here serves the oracle.

## 3. Terms — used consistently across every document in this set

The deck touches four traditions, each with its own vocabulary. To avoid
borrowing one tradition's word for a general idea, these terms are fixed:

- **Card** — the object: the thing in the deck, the page in the oracle.
- **Code** — the *meaning* a card carries: the one unified energy that all
  four lineages point at. The deck's own term. Reader-facing and internal.
  When a guide says "the energy of the code," it means this.
- **Hexagram** — used *only* for the I-Ching layer (the 64 hexagrams).
- **Gate** — used *only* for the literal Human Design gate (Gate 1, Gate 41).
  Never used loosely to mean "the card's energy" — that is the *code*.
- **Shadow / Gift / Siddhi** — used *only* for the Gene Keys frequencies.
- **Arcana** — used *only* for the Tarot mapping.

The word "key" is avoided as a general term — it belongs to Gene Keys, and
using it loosely blurs the copyright line. Each lineage's vocabulary stays in
its own lineage.

## 4. The anatomy of a card — what gets written

In reading order on the card:

| Part | What it is | Who governs it |
|---|---|---|
| **Card name** | The poetic title (e.g. "Beyond the Shell") | This guide, §9 |
| **Keywords** | 5–7 brief words under the art — the at-a-glance pinpoint | `05_KEYWORDS_GUIDE.md` |
| **I-Ching section** | The hexagram, in three layers | `03_ICHING_GUIDE.md` |
| **Description** | The Shadow / Gift / Siddhi prose, the teaching | `01_DESCRIPTION_SPEC.md` |
| **Invocation** | The spoken alignment passage, the transmission | `02_INVOCATION_GUIDE.md` |

The **description teaches** — the reader understands the code.
The **invocation transmits** — the reader briefly becomes the code.
They are two different organs of the same card and are written differently.
For how the parts relate, and what each may *not* repeat, see §5.

## 5. Card coherence — the whole card as one thing

A reader meets a card as a single object, top to bottom. The four parts must
form one experience, not four assembled pieces saying the same thing four
times. This section is the doctrine of the whole card. It is the most
important section in this guide.

### 5.1 What each part uniquely contributes

Each part has one job no other part does. If two parts are doing the same job,
one of them is wrong.

| Part | Its unique job | What it must NOT do |
|---|---|---|
| **Card name** | Hold the code as a single image | Explain. A name names. |
| **Keywords** | *Pinpoint* the code at a glance — instant, no framework needed | Use any system's jargon, or anything the reader must decode |
| **I-Ching intro** | Name the *life-situation* — the human moment | State the natural image (that is the interpretation's job) |
| **I-Ching classic** | Carry the *lineage* — the ancient transmission | Be modernized or explained inline |
| **I-Ching interpretation** | Unlock the *natural image* — mountain over lake, what moves toward what | Re-state the life-situation or drift into the Gene Keys frequencies |
| **Description** | Teach the *three frequencies* — Shadow, Gift, Siddhi as a movement | Re-explain the hexagram or the natural image |
| **Invocation** | *Transmit* — let the reader speak the code in first person | Summarize anything above it; explain |

The natural image belongs to the I-Ching interpretation. The frequencies
belong to the description. The life-situation belongs to the I-Ching intro.
When you find yourself writing the natural image into the description, or the
frequencies into the interpretation, stop — that material has a home, and it
is not here.

### 5.2 The one repetition that is allowed

One thread *should* run through every part: the **one-breath truth** of the
code (see §6, step 1). Every part expresses it — but each in its own register.
The name images it, the intro situates it, the description teaches it, the
invocation speaks it. That is coherence, not repetition: the same truth, four
instruments. Repetition is when two parts express it the *same way*.

### 5.3 Finding the one energy when the lineages diverge

The guides repeatedly say "find the one energy all four lineages point at."
This is not always easy, and the guides must be honest about that. The four
mappings were assembled by different traditions; for some cards they converge
cleanly, for others they pull in noticeably different directions (a Gene Keys
Gift and a Human Design keyword can emphasize different things).

Procedure when they diverge:

1. **Look for the deeper root.** Most apparent divergence is two traditions
   describing the same energy at different altitudes — one names the feeling,
   one names the function. Find the root both grow from. That root is usually
   the one-breath truth.
2. **If they genuinely will not reconcile, choose a lead.** Decide which
   lineage most truly carries this card's energy, lead the one-breath truth
   from it, and let the others sit as facets rather than forcing a false
   merge. Log which lineage you led from and why.
3. **Never paper over the tension with a vague abstraction.** A one-breath
   truth so general it "includes" all four ("this card is about
   transformation") is a failure, not a synthesis. Specific and slightly
   imperfect beats vague and seamless.

A small amount of honest tension between the lineages is not a flaw in the
card. It is the texture of a real object. Do not sand it away.

### 5.4 Human Design and Tarot — their status

I-Ching and Gene Keys are *written-out parts* of the card (their own sections,
their own guides). **Human Design and Tarot are not written-out sections.**
They are mapping lineages: they inform the one-breath truth, they feed the
keyword synthesis (see `05` — the keywords distil *all four* lineages), they
may surface as *felt texture* in the invocation (the gate's location in the
body, the Arcana's gesture — see `02`), and they may appear as quiet metadata
on the card (gate number, Arcana name). They are not given their own prose
section and do not need their own guide. §4's anatomy table is the full list
of written parts; HD and Tarot are background mappings, named honestly as
such.

This is also why the keyword layer matters. The keywords are the one place
the synthesis of the whole code becomes *visible and primary* on the card.
They are judged by one test only — does each keyword hold the energy of the
card — and never by which system it came from. See §5.1 and `05`.

## 6. The copyright line (applies to every part)

Two separate things keep getting confused. Keep them apart.

### KEEP — the system names and the architecture

The Shadow / Gift / Siddhi **name triads**, the hexagram names, the Human
Design gate names and keywords, the Tarot Arcana — these are the **spine of
the deck and they stay.** Permission is being sought for the Gene Keys names,
which is the correct path. Do not strip the names.

The teaching *architectures* are also kept and are nobody's property: the
three-frequency model (Vedanta, alchemy, Jung all predate Rudd), the
repressed/reactive polarity (Jungian shadow-work), the I-Ching itself, the
trigrams — all public.

### CUT — decorative flourishes and borrowed prose

Cut any phrase that fails **either** test:
- **(a) You cannot verify the mapping yourself.** Phrases that assert
  connections that are not established teaching, dropped in to sound profound.
  (E.g. "the Promethean fire" for the Siddhi of Beauty — unverified.)
- **(b) The wording is a source's, not yours.** A teacher's specific coined
  prose — not the names, the *prose inventions*. (E.g. "the genetic wheel of
  samsara," "the single-celled heaven.")

Either failure and the phrase comes out: wrong, or borrowed. An image survives
only if the mapping is true *and* the words are yours *and* it serves the card.

### NEVER — name a source in the writing

Never write a teacher's name, a book title, or a framework's name in card
prose or invocation. Convey the concept as if you arrived at it yourself.

**The working rule for everything:** observation, not definition. Write what
you have *seen* this energy do. Never paraphrase a source.

## 7. Shared voice rules (every part of every card)

These hold for the card name, the I-Ching section, the description, and the
invocation. (They bind both phases — see §1.)

- **No em dashes.** Commas, periods, line breaks. This is a site-wide rule and
  applies to all card copy. (These guide documents themselves use em dashes
  freely; they are internal working documents, not card copy. The rule is for
  what ships on a card.)
- **No adjective triads.** "Lost, unmoored, wandering" becomes one precise
  word.
- **"It is not X, it is Y"** — once per card maximum. Repeated, it becomes a
  mechanical cadence.
- **Vary sentence and line length hard.** A three-word sentence beside a long
  one. Fragments are allowed.
- **Ground the abstract.** Not "the feeling of being lost" but who feels it,
  when, doing what.
- **Trust the image.** Do not explain a metaphor after using it.
- **Plain words over spiritual vocabulary.** "Light," "energy," "divine,"
  "frequency" — rare, and only when nothing plainer will carry it.
- **No source naming.** Ever (see §6).
- **Read it aloud.** If a line clangs, if you run out of breath, if your own
  body does not shift, rewrite it.

### How to keep the writing human

The reason this matters: the deck must read as written by a person who has
*lived* these 64 codes, not assembled by a system. So the test is not a list
of banned sentence shapes. It is one quality, present or absent:

**Lived particularity.** A human writer reaches for the specific detail that
only their own experience would supply — a real Tuesday afternoon, a particular
room, the exact small thing a person does when caught in a Shadow. Generated
prose reaches for the *category* instead: "a feeling of disconnection," "a
sense of unease." The category is plausible and empty. The particular is
unmistakable and true.

Two practical consequences:

- **Write from a real instance, then generalize — never the reverse.** Before
  describing a Shadow, recall an actual time you or someone you watched lived
  it. Write that. The general truth will be visible *through* the particular;
  it does not need to be stated.
- **Distrust the fluent first draft.** The smoothest version of a sentence is
  often the most generic. If a passage came easily and sounds finished, check
  it for a single concrete, specific, unexpected detail. If there is none, it
  is probably a category dressed as an observation. Cut it and write the
  instance.

This is the Phase 2 standard at full strength. In Phase 1, AI cannot draw on
lived memory — so Phase 1 writes the most concrete, specific, non-generic
version it can, and marks the card as scaffold (see §10) precisely because
this quality is the thing Adrian must later supply.

## 8. The workflow — one card, blank to finished

Do the parts in this order. Each feeds the next.

1. **Sit with the whole card.** Read all four mappings — hexagram, Gene Keys
   triad, HD gate, Tarot. Find the **one energy they all point at** and write
   it as a single sentence: the card's *one-breath truth*. Everything else is
   built from this sentence. If the lineages diverge rather than converge,
   follow the procedure in §5.3 — do not force a vague merge.

2. **Confirm or refine the card name.** Does the poetic name still hold the
   one-breath truth? (See §9.)

3. **Write the I-Ching section.** Follow `03_ICHING_GUIDE.md`. Three layers:
   intro, the rendered classic, the interpretation.

4. **Write the description.** Follow `01_DESCRIPTION_SPEC.md`. Opening line,
   then Shadow / Gift / Siddhi paragraphs.

5. **Fill the invocation working sheet, then write the invocation.** Follow
   `02_INVOCATION_GUIDE.md`. The invocation is the last *prose* written,
   because it is the distillation of everything above it.

6. **Distil the keywords.** Follow `05_KEYWORDS_GUIDE.md`. 5–7 brief words
   that pinpoint the whole code at a glance. Written last, because they
   distil the finished card — they synthesize all four systems, never copy
   one.

7. **Check coherence.** Re-read the whole card against §5: does each part do
   its unique job, and only its job? Is the one-breath truth present in
   every register without any two parts repeating each other?

8. **Run the checklists.** The per-part checklists at the end of `01`, `02`,
   `03`, `04`, and `05`, plus the shared rules in §6 and §7 here.

9. **Read the whole card aloud, top to bottom.** Name, keywords, I-Ching
   section, description, invocation. It should feel like one voice describing
   one thing.

10. **Set the card's status** (see §10).

## 9. The card name

A short poetic title (2–4 words) that holds the one-breath truth in an image.
Not the hexagram name, not the Gift name — a *third thing*, Adrian's, that the
other mappings live inside. "Beyond the Shell," "Ancestors Bloom," "Earth's
Breath." Standard: concrete image, no abstraction, obeys §7. Most names exist
already; this is for review and the occasional rewrite.

The I-Ching section has its own document — see `03_ICHING_GUIDE.md`.

## 10. Card status — tracking the two phases

Every card has a status, so scaffold work (Phase 1) and finished work
(Phase 2) are never confused. Three values:

- **`scaffold`** — written by AI in Phase 1. Complete, coherent, on-standard,
  but not yet Adrian's own. Awaiting Phase 2.
- **`in-progress`** — Adrian is actively reworking this card.
- **`final`** — Adrian has personally written or rewritten the card and
  considers it done.

**The status is internal and never reader-facing.** It lives in a tracking
file or a metadata field that does not render. This is deliberate: the deck
must *present as a complete, finished work at every stage*, even mid-Phase-2.
A reader, or an early collaborator drawing the cards, should never see "draft,"
never feel a gap, never know which cards are scaffold. The provisional-ness is
a fact for Adrian's process only.

This is what lets the deck be three things at once: a complete oracle anyone
can use now, a study instrument Adrian uses to tune into each code so he can
write his own version, and a long-running art project completed one card at a
time. The scaffold makes the whole deck usable and studyable immediately; the
status field makes sure Phase 2 actually gets finished.

---

## 11. The deck as a study instrument

Beyond producing the cards, this guide set has a second use: Adrian studies it,
and the scaffolded cards, to tune deeply into each code — so that Phase 2
writing comes from genuine contemplation rather than editing. Read this way,
the per-card material (the one-breath truth, the working sheets in `02` and
`04`, the contemplation prompts) is not just production scaffolding. It is a
map for sitting with a code until it becomes yours. The same documents that
make the deck may also be inspiring to others later; they are written to hold
up to that.
