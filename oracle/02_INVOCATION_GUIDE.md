# 02 — The Invocation Guide

How to write the invocation on all 64 cards.

Read `00_MASTER_WRITING_GUIDE.md` first. The master guide holds the two-phase
frame (§1), the terms (§3), the card-coherence doctrine (§5), the copyright
line (§6), the shared voice rules (§7) and the human-writing discipline — all
of which apply here and are not repeated. This document covers only what is
specific to the **invocation**. Per master §5.1, the invocation's unique job
is to *transmit* — it never summarizes or explains the parts above it.

The invocation **transmits** — the reader briefly becomes the code. (Its
companion, the description, *teaches* — see `01`.) This is the part of the
deck that exists nowhere else. It is what makes the deck unlike anything
adjacent to it.

This guide is not a generator. The invocation must be pulled from Adrian's own
seeing. The guide exists so that when you sit to write one, you already have a
strong, consolidated understanding of the whole card, and a true statement to
draw from.

---

## 1. What the invocation is — and the key principle

A card is a single **code**, mapped across four lineages: the I-Ching
hexagram, the Gene Keys triad, the Human Design gate, the Tarot Arcana. (See
master guide §1.)

**The invocation aligns the reader with the whole code.** Not the Gene Keys
frequency alone — the entire card, as one unified thing.

The principle that makes this work:

> The four lineages are four views of one energy. The invocation speaks
> **that one energy** — it does not tour the four systems.

An invocation that went "here is the hexagram, here is the Gift, here is the
gate, here is the Arcana" would be four things stapled together, twelve lines
long, dead on arrival. The four mappings are how the card was *charted*. The
invocation is what the card *is*. No system is named. No system gets its own
line. They dissolve into the single energy they were all describing, and the
reader speaks that.

### What an invocation is not

- **Not an affirmation** ("I am abundant"). Affirmations assert a state the
  reader may not be in; the gap breeds quiet self-distrust.
- **Not a summary** of the description. The reader has just read that.
- **Not an instruction** ("let go of fear"). Instructions are received by the
  mind, not the body.

An invocation **is** a short spoken passage that walks the reader through the
card's own movement — recognition, embodiment, opening — so that by the last
line they are standing, briefly, inside the code. A doorway, not a description
of a doorway.

## 2. The form (locked)

- **Voice:** first person. The reader speaks it as themselves.
- **Shape:** a descent — recognition → embodiment → opening.
- **Length:** 5 to 9 lines. One held breath of attention.
- **Each line:** short. Speakable aloud without running out of breath.

### The core principle: true from anywhere

A reader does not always draw a card while in the Shadow. They may draw it
standing in the Gift. They may draw it as confirmation. They may be reading
the deck straight through. So every line must be **honest no matter where the
reader is standing.** The invocation can never force the reader to declare a
state they are not in — not a Shadow they have escaped, not a Gift they have
not reached.

### The descent, line by line

The descent is the movement of the *whole card*, not the Gene Keys
frequencies. But the card's energy still has a low expression, a lived
expression, and a luminous expression — so the three movements are:

**Opening lines — recognition and readiness.**
The reader recognizes the card's low expression as something known, and
declares a readiness to learn from it. Recognition is honest from anywhere: if
you are in it you recognize it now; if you have moved past it you recognize
where it once moved or still tugs; reading through, you recognize it as human.
Readiness turns the line from a report of a problem into an orientation —
active, dignified, no shame. Pattern:

> I recognize where I have [the low movement of this code],
> and I am ready to learn what this code would teach me.

2 lines, occasionally 1 if it compresses cleanly.

**Middle lines — embodiment, as present-tense action.**
The lived expression of the code, as a verb the reader can do *as they speak
it*: "I turn the way water turns." Not "I will be," not "I am" (a state) — a
present-tense action, embodiable in the moment of speaking. This is the body
of the invocation, and the place the four lineages fuse most tightly: the
hexagram's motion, the Gift's verb, the gate's bodily pressure, the Arcana's
gesture, all spoken as one act. 2 to 4 lines.

**Closing line(s) — the opening, as the lightest touch.**
The luminous expression of the code, never claimed. A line that points past
the self, often an image, often very quiet. The reader closes on a held
breath, not a full stop. 1 to 2 lines.

### Why this works from anywhere

- The recognition line *recognizes* (true whether you are in it or remember
  it) and declares *readiness* (true for anyone willing — anyone who drew the
  card).
- The embodiment line is an *action*, doable in the moment of speaking.
- The opening line *does not assert* — it opens toward a horizon, claims
  nothing.

The reader never speaks a false sentence, in either direction. The structure
earns every word before the mouth says it.

### Keep it personal

The invocation stays between the reader and the code. Do not weave in a
"witnessed by others" note. The reader's embodiment may ripple outward, but
the invocation does not assert that relational stance — it keeps the address
simple and universal: the reader, the code, and what the code would teach.

## 3. How to understand a card well enough to write its invocation

Before writing, sit with the *whole card* — all four mappings — and answer
these for yourself, as raw notes. The invocation gets pulled from them.

**Finding the one energy:**
- Read the hexagram, the Gene Keys triad, the HD gate, the Tarot Arcana. What
  single energy are all four pointing at? Write it as one sentence. This is
  the *one-breath truth* and everything is built from it.
- Where do the four mappings *agree*? That agreement is the core of the card.
- Does any mapping add an image or a body-location the others do not? (The
  hexagram's natural image, the gate's center in the body, the Arcana's
  gesture.) These become felt texture in the invocation — never named, but
  woven in.

**On the low expression (recognition opener):**
- When have *I* met this energy at its low expression? Name an actual time.
  You write from your contact; the reader speaks it from theirs.
- What is the *movement* of it — the thing a person does when caught here?
  Phrase it to follow "where I have...": "where I have drifted," "where I have
  grasped," "where I have gone silent." That phrase is the opening line.

**On the lived expression (embodiment middle):**
- What does this energy look like as a *verb* — something a person does, not a
  quality they have? That verb is the spine of the middle lines.
- Have I seen it move, in someone or in myself, even briefly? Describe the
  moment. The middle lines come from this.
- What is its smallest, most ordinary form? Invocations land when the energy
  is reachable, not heroic.

**On the luminous expression (opening close):**
- What is true here that I cannot *do*, only *allow*?
- What single image holds it? The closing line is almost always an image.
- Can I say something honest about it at all? If not, the closing line should
  be very short and very quiet. Never decorate a height you cannot yet see.

## 4. The working sheet

For each card, fill this before writing. Keep the sheets in one file beside
the JSON (a blank 64-sheet file can be generated on request).

```
CARD [n] — [card name]
Hexagram:   [name]            Gene Keys:  [Shadow / Gift / Siddhi]
HD gate:    [gate, keyword]   Tarot:      [Arcana]

One-breath truth:   [the single energy all four mappings point at]
Where they agree:   [the core the four mappings share]
Felt textures:      [image / body-location / gesture from any mapping]
My contact:         [an actual memory of meeting this energy]
Low movement:       ["where I have ___" — what a person does at the low end]
The verb:           [the lived energy as an action the reader DOES]
Smallest form:      [the ordinary, reachable version]
Opening image:      [one image for the close, or "leave quiet"]
```

The invocation is then an act of compression: the low movement becomes the
recognition opener, the verb becomes the embodiment middle, the opening image
becomes the close — with the felt textures woven through.

## 5. Worked example — Card 2, "Beyond the Shell"

Hexagram: K'un / Receptive · Gene Keys: Dislocation / Orientation / Unity
HD gate: 2, Direction · Tarot: carried at the codon-ring level

**Working sheet:**
```
One-breath truth:   Direction is a thing the body finds, not the mind chooses.
Where they agree:   All four point at orientation arising from within, not from a map.
Felt textures:      water finding its way down; the body turning like a plant
My contact:         The years of feeling the right place was elsewhere.
Low movement:       where I have sought my place outside myself
The verb:           to turn (toward), to feel-the-way
Smallest form:      noticing which way you already lean
Opening image:      no distance left to cross
```

**Invocation:**
> I recognize where I have sought my place outside myself,
> and I am ready to learn what this code would teach me.
> I set down the map I made from fear.
> I turn the way water turns, before the reason comes.
> I trust the sense that once felt like being lost.
> There is no wrong place. There never was a distance.

Six lines. Recognition and readiness (lines 1–2, honest from anywhere),
embodiment as present-tense action (lines 3–5, the verb "turn" with the water
texture woven in), opening as the lightest touch (line 6, an image, no claim).
No lineage is named; the one energy is spoken.

## 6. Invocation-specific voice notes

The shared voice rules in master guide §7 all apply. Notes specific to the
invocation:

- **Vary the music across the 64.** Same structure, different rhythm. Some
  invocations open hard and short, some soft. The skeleton is fixed; the music
  is not. The biggest risk across 64 cards is sameness.
- **The opening line is the easiest to fake.** If you cannot say something
  true and luminous, say something short. A quiet honest close beats a
  decorated one.
- **Read every invocation aloud before it is done.** If you run out of breath,
  if a line clangs, if your own body does not shift even slightly, rewrite it.
  This test matters more for the invocation than for any other part — the
  invocation is meant to be spoken.

## 7. Per-invocation checklist

- [ ] First person throughout.
- [ ] Speaks the one energy of the whole card. No lineage named or sectioned.
- [ ] Opens with recognition and readiness — honest whether the reader is in
      the low expression, past it, or just reading through. Never forces a
      present state.
- [ ] Middle moves to embodiment as present-tense action (a verb, not a
      state), with the card's felt textures woven in.
- [ ] Closes toward the luminous expression without claiming it, usually on an
      image.
- [ ] 5 to 9 short, speakable lines.
- [ ] No false sentence anywhere — nothing the reader cannot honestly say,
      from anywhere in the card.
- [ ] Stays personal — no "witnessed by others" note.
- [ ] Master guide §6 (copyright) and §7 (voice) obeyed.
- [ ] Read aloud. The body shifts.
- [ ] Card status set (master §10): `scaffold` if AI-drafted, `final` once
      Adrian has personally written or reworked it.
