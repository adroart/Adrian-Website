# 03 — The I-Ching Guide

How to write the I-Ching section on all 64 cards.

Read `00_MASTER_WRITING_GUIDE.md` first. The master guide holds the two-phase
frame (§1), the terms (§3), the card-coherence doctrine (§5), the copyright
line (§6), the shared voice rules (§7) and the human-writing discipline — all
of which apply here and are not repeated. This document covers only what is
specific to the **I-Ching section**. Per master §5.1, the three layers have
distinct jobs and must not repeat each other (see §3 below).

The I-Ching section carries the deepest lineage on the card — a text three
thousand years old. The design problem it solves: keep that lineage **whole
and authoritative**, while making the card **reachable** for a reader who has
never met the I-Ching. The answer is not to modernize the classic text. It is
to layer.

---

## 1. The principle: layer, do not blend

Blending is the wrong move. "Lightly modernizing" the classic text damages the
lineage (it stops sounding ancient, loses its authority) without fully
clarifying it (it stays somewhat opaque). A muddy middle, worst of both.

Instead the section has **three layers, shallow to deep**, and each layer is
**independently complete**. A reader can stop at any layer and have received
something whole. Nobody is forced through the archaic text to find value, and
nobody has the archaic text withheld or diluted. The reader chooses their own
depth.

This is also the right reverence: the classic is *honored* by being set apart
and preserved intact, not by being smoothed into the surrounding prose.

---

## 2. The three layers

In order, top to bottom on the card:

### Layer 1 — The Intro (your voice; the situation)

A short plain-voice welcome. Its job: **name the life-situation** the hexagram
describes. When a reader draws a card they have a question; the intro meets
that question in human terms. "This is the moment when something must
decrease before it can grow." "This is the situation of pure beginning,
before anything has taken form."

- Typically 2 to 4 sentences, around 40 to 70 words. A typical range, not a
  gate — see the note at the end of this section.
- Plain, warm, reachable. No archaic register, no jargon.
- Situation-focused, not image-focused (the image belongs in Layer 3).
- A reader in a hurry gets what they need here and can stop. The intro does
  not depend on the layers below it.

### Layer 2 — The Classic (rendered; the transmission)

The ancient text, preserved and set apart. Treated like an inscription — the
reader understands they are looking at something old, and part of its job is
simply *to be old*. This is the lineage layer.

It holds two traditional elements, kept distinct:
- **The Judgment** — the terse oracular pronouncement on the hexagram.
- **The Image** — the statement of the two trigrams and the stance they
  counsel ("Thus the superior person...").

- Terse, compressed, ancient in register. Do not smooth it.
- Set apart visually from your prose (see §5).
- This layer does not depend on the others. A reader may receive it as pure
  transmission without the interpretation.

**Sourcing — render it yourself, from the source.** The specific English
translations (Wilhelm/Baynes, Huang, and others) are each copyrighted — the
wording belongs to the translator. The classic layer is therefore a **fresh
rendering of the Judgment and Image, made by Adrian** from the Chinese source,
triangulated across multiple scholarly readings.

This is a serious scholarly act with its own full method — see
`04_TRANSLATION_METHOD.md`. In brief: AI triangulates the Chinese characters
and multiple scholarly translations and flags every disagreement; Adrian forms
his own sense of each line before seeing any draft; AI then drafts a synthesis;
Adrian writes the final line and logs every contested choice. The wording is
Adrian's, so it is clean; the result is terse and classic in register; the
Chinese hexagram name and trigram names are kept as given.

### Layer 3 — The Interpretation (your voice; the image)

Your voice again, deeper. Its job: **unlock the natural image** the hexagram
is built on, and open what the terse classic text was compressing. This is
the reward for the reader who descended past the classic.

- Explain the trigram picture — mountain over lake, fire over heaven — and
  what that image *does*. What is above, what is below, what moves toward
  what.
- Open the compression of the Judgment and Image: why decrease here, what the
  counselled stance protects.
- Typically 3 to 5 sentences, around 60 to 100 words. A typical range, not a
  gate — see the note below.
- Plain voice, but it may go deeper and more contemplative than the intro.
- This layer does not depend on being read — the reader who stopped at Layer 1
  or 2 has lost nothing structural.

### On the length figures

The figures for Layer 1 and Layer 3 are *typical ranges, not gates*. They keep
the 64 cards consistent and give a quick check. If a hexagram's situation or
image genuinely needs more or less, that is allowed — but ask why first. The
classic layer (Layer 2) has no word figure at all: its length is whatever the
Judgment and Image honestly require, no padding to fill and no cutting to fit.

---

## 3. Why the jobs are split this way

- **Intro = situation.** Accessibility lives at the top. The person in need
  gets the human meaning first, with no archaic gate in front of it.
- **Classic = transmission.** The lineage is preserved whole, in the middle,
  set apart and untouched.
- **Interpretation = image.** The natural image is the deep structure of the
  hexagram; it rewards the reader who came down for it. Image as the reward of
  depth, not the entryway.

Accessibility at the top, tradition preserved in the middle, depth at the
bottom. The reader drops in at whatever layer they want and every layer is
whole.

---

## 4. Worked example — Card 41 (Hexagram of Decrease)

The current JSON essence reads, in part: *"Diminishing. Possessing conformity.
Spring, significant. Without fault, permitting Trial..."* — a stitched-together
translation a reader bounces off. Here is the same hexagram, layered.

**Layer 1 — Intro:**
> This is the moment of decrease. Something is being asked to grow smaller, to
> give up a part of itself, to send energy away rather than gather it in. It
> can feel like loss. It is the contraction that makes a true beginning
> possible.

**Layer 2 — Classic** (rendered, set apart):
> *The Judgment.* Decrease. Held with sincerity, it brings deep fortune. No
> blame. One may persist. There is somewhere worth going. How is the offering
> made? Two small bowls are enough.
>
> *The Image.* Below the mountain, the lake. The mountain takes; the lake
> gives. Thus the noble one curbs anger and stills desire.

**Layer 3 — Interpretation:**
> The picture is a lake at the foot of a mountain. The lake gives its moisture
> upward as mist, and the mountain stands taller for it. What is below offers
> itself to what is above, and the offering is willing, not extracted. The
> two small bowls matter: when the offering is sincere, it need not be large.
> Decrease here is not poverty. It is the deliberate releasing of what is in
> the way, so the new thing has room to begin.

Three layers. A reader can take only the intro and understand the card. A
reader can sit with only the classic. A reader can descend the whole way.

---

## 5. Presentation note

The three layers must be **visually distinct** so the reader can see the
depth structure and choose where to enter:
- The intro and interpretation are your prose — set in the card's body style.
- The classic layer is set apart — a different treatment (indented, a quieter
  or italic face, an inscription style). The Judgment and the Image should
  read as two distinct elements within it.
- Labels ("The Judgment," "The Image") may be quietly present so the reader
  knows the traditional structure they are looking at.

The exact styling is a card-design decision. The writing requirement is only
that the three layers are clearly separable, never run together as one block.

---

## 6. Per-card I-Ching checklist

- [ ] Layer 1 intro names the life-situation, plain voice, near the typical
      range (~40–70 words), and stands complete on its own.
- [ ] Layer 2 classic holds a Judgment and an Image, kept distinct, terse and
      ancient in register.
- [ ] Layer 2 is rendered following `04_TRANSLATION_METHOD.md` in full — its
      per-hexagram checklist is satisfied, the worksheet kept.
- [ ] Layer 3 interpretation unlocks the trigram image and opens the
      compression of the classic, near the typical range (~60–100 words).
- [ ] Each layer is independently complete — a reader stopping early loses
      nothing structural.
- [ ] Chinese hexagram name and trigram names kept as given.
- [ ] Each layer does only its own job (master §5.1) — intro names the
      situation, interpretation unlocks the image; neither poaches the other.
- [ ] Master guide §6 (copyright) and §7 (voice) obeyed.
- [ ] All three layers read aloud.
- [ ] Card status set (master §10): `scaffold` if AI-drafted, `final` once
      Adrian has personally written or reworked it.
